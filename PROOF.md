# A candidate computer-assisted proof of C(14,5,3)=43

Audited research draft, 24 November 2026. **Proposed theorem: C(14,5,3)=43.** The proof uses exhaustive finite enumeration and integer certificates. A fresh complete verification of the original archive finished successfully (159.59 seconds in the audit environment; see `logs/audit_full_verify.log`). Additional checks strengthen input validation and per-representative accounting, and a separately implemented residual verifier rejects all 8,641 surviving regular unions. External expert review, formalization, and priority verification are pending. The earlier source documents and interrupted-run logs are historical intermediate results; this document supersedes their statements that the exact value is unresolved.

## 1. Setting, upper bound, and removal lemma

Let V have 14 points. A covering is a family of five-subsets covering every triple. It suffices to consider distinct blocks: duplicates can be discarded. The 43 distinct masks in `two_low/upper43_masks.txt` cover all 364 triples, as checked by `two_low/verify_new.py`. This witness is a relabeling of the historical Nurmela–Östergård construction; see `data/UPPER_WITNESS_PROVENANCE.md`. No novelty is claimed for the upper bound. For a hypothetical covering of size at most 42 write d(x) for the number of blocks through x and lambda(S) for the multiplicity of a subset S. The link at x consists of four-sets on 13 points covering all 78 pairs, so d(x)>=13. Similarly lambda(x,y)>=4. If d(x)=13, every link pair occurs exactly once and every link point has degree four.

We independently establish the removal lemma for degree 14, previously used from Gardiner's work. In a 14-block link every link point has degree at least four and the excesses above four sum to 4. Choose a point of excess zero. Its four blocks partition the other 12 points into four triples. Relabel the point as 12, and the triples as consecutive triples of labels 0,...,11. Sorting weights within and between groups gives all 13 normalized weight layouts. The remaining ten four-sets must avoid point 12. `core/verify_degree14.py` enumerates all such completions, covering pairs incident to zero-weight points exactly once and other pairs at least once, tracking exact degrees. It obtains 280 labelled completions across the 13 layouts. In every completion there is a four-set all six of whose pairs occur at least twice. Deleting it leaves an exact 13-block link. This proves the removal lemma by finite enumeration, without an external mathematical lemma. Because the six excess pair incidences in a 14-block link are all used by that four-set, its six pairs are exactly the doubled pairs and all other pairs occur once.

## 2. Three or more low points, and the lower bound 42

Call a point low if its degree is 13 or 14. For a degree-14 point let its special parent block be the block corresponding to the removable link block. A degree-13 point cannot belong to a special block: the triples through that point would be doubled. If a degree-14 point y belongs to the special block of another degree-14 point x, the three triples through xy in that block are doubled. The special block at y must contain all five of the same points, so the two special blocks agree. Consequently one can simultaneously delete the distinct special blocks for any selected low points, leaving exact links at all selected points. All blocks in their exact links belonged to the original covering.

The proof and executable verification in `two_low/PREVIOUS_PROOF.md` and `verify_new.py` enumerate three exact links. Two roots have four shared blocks; their other nine blocks each are transversals of the four common triples. There are 72 possibilities for the second transversal family after fixing the first by relabeling. Exhaustive exact cover of the missing link pairs gives 96 third-link completions, each a fixed family of 28 parent blocks. The remaining uncovered triples define h(P), the number through a pair P. Residual pair degrees are at least ceil(h(P)/3), and residual point degrees are at least ceil(sum_{P contains x} ceil(h(P)/3)/4). The sum of these point lower bounds is 72 in 72 cases and 74 in 24 cases. At most 14 further blocks give only 70 incidences. Thus no covering of size at most 42 has three low points. This count remains valid if deleted special blocks belong to the remaining blocks.

A covering with at most 41 blocks has point degree sum at most 205. If it had at most two low points, that sum would be at least 12*15+2*13=206. Hence such a covering is impossible. A minimal covering with 42 blocks is therefore simple, and its degree sum is 210.

## 3. Reduction to exact low points and exclusion of two low points

If one or two low points are present, delete their special blocks as above. Replace each deleted block by a five-set avoiding the chosen low points and containing all of its unselected points. This preserves all triples outside the selected roots, and the exact links cover all triples touching the roots. There are enough unselected points to pad every set. A repeated replacement would yield a covering with at most 41 distinct blocks, already excluded. Unselected point degrees do not decrease. Thus we may reduce to exactly one or exactly two low points, all of degree 13.

For two exact roots, the 22 fixed parent blocks have five relative types of second transversal family, with orbit sizes 27,24,12,8,1. `two_low/verify_local.py` independently checks the orbit classification and 143 integer inequalities certificates. The argument is detailed in `two_low/NEW_PROOF.md`. Types 2 and 4 force more than four high points; type 0 forces exactly four specified high points of degree 16, leaving eight points of degree 15. The exact local enumeration `two_low/direct_local.py` produces 1522 possible local continuations at a representative of those eight points. The explicitly verified relabelings transport the domain to the other seven. `compat_all.py` enforces agreement on every common parent block, not merely counts, and enumerates the 36 consistent assignments. Each uses 22 residual blocks, but only 20 are allowed. Type 0 is impossible. See `two_low/TYPE0_PROOF.md`.

For type 1 there are two orbits of sizes 9 and 3, with local domain sizes 1522 and 140. At least eight of the twelve points must have degree 15 because the total degree surplus is four. For each of the 495 eight-point choices, enforcing pairwise equality of the complete common-block signatures empties a domain. For type 3 the nine-point orbit has domain size 140; at least five of its points have degree 15. All 126 five-point choices similarly fail. `two_low/verify_all_subsets.py --fresh` regenerates the local domains using `local.py`. These searches are exhaustive since every removal from a domain is a choice with no compatible choice at another required point. Details are in Section 2 of `two_low/COMBINED_PRIOR_PROOF.md`. Thus no 42-cover has two low points.

## 4. Exhaustive local classification at degree 15

A 15-block link on 13 points has point degrees 4+w(z), where w>=0 and sum w=8. Choose a point of weight zero (there is always one) and normalize its four incident blocks to the four groups, as in Section 1. There are 143 sorted layouts of the remaining weights. `core/one_low_input.py` generates them; `verify.py` checks equality against the independent generator in `verify_degree14.py`.

`core/enum_reps.cpp` enumerates the 11 remaining four-sets. Pairs touching a zero-weight point must occur exactly once. Pairs with two positive endpoints need coverage at least once. Branch on an uncovered exact pair, considering every admissible four-set containing it; when no such pair remains, enumerate every distinct set of additional all-positive blocks. Track all exact point degrees and the block budget. Every pruning bound is necessary. This partitions all completions (no repeated block with a nonempty exact-pair mask is possible). The group of within-group and between-group permutations preserving the weight layout acts on the completions; remove each full orbit, checking that every image is present. Thus the representatives are exhaustive, without assuming symmetry of a full covering.

## 5. Exclusion of exactly one low point

After Section 3 assume x has degree 13 and is the only low point. All other points have degree at least 15, with total surplus exactly two. Therefore at least eleven of them have degree 15. If any degree-15 point y has a removable link block, its parent block cannot contain x, because triples containing x have multiplicity one. Replace that block by a five-set avoiding x,y and retaining its four points other than y. This preserves coverage and gives two low points (13 and 14). A duplicate replacement gives at most 41 distinct blocks. Both outcomes are impossible. Thus every degree-15 link in this branch is nonremovable.

Choose any degree-15 point y. Pair xy has codegree four. Its four shared blocks partition the twelve other points Q into four triples. Use x=12, y=13 and the representatives of Section 4 (the zero-weight point in y's link is x). The remaining nine x-blocks are an exact transversal family of these groups, with all 72 possibilities generated. Pair each representative of y's link with all 72 x-link families. This produces 54,144 cases (752*72); the relabeling used to normalize y's link simply moves the x-link to another of those 72 families. `core/one_low_all.cpp` enumerates these cases. Their union F consists of 24 blocks, leaving 18 five-sets on Q.

For each pair P in Q, let p(P) be its fixed multiplicity and h(P) its missing triple count. A residual pair degree must be at least max(0,4-p(P),ceil(h(P)/3)). At each point z the residual degree is at least the maximum of 15-d_F(z), ceil(missing triples through z / 6), and ceil(sum of pair lower bounds through z / 4). The sum of these lower bounds cannot exceed 90. This eliminates 27,712 cases. Of the 26,432 remaining, 10,091 have a removable block in the y-link and are impossible by the preceding argument. The other 16,341 cases are checked as follows.

### Signature table

For a possible degree-15 point z in Q, its four blocks through x are fixed and partition V minus {x,z} into four triples. The blocks through y and z are also completely fixed, because y's link is complete. The unique block through x,y,z is already among the four; the other blocks through y,z give a list of triples after removing y,z. Normalize the group containing y first, with y marked at position zero. A signature is the sorted list of these triples, with the other labels in the four groups. The group preserving this partition and the marked point has order 2*6^3*3!=2592. `core/signature_all.cpp` inserts every image under this group of every signature from each of the 410 nonremovable representatives of Section 4, for every choice of marked point other than the distinguished zero-weight point. There are 352,008 distinct labelled signatures. The integer key is an injective base-256 encoding of sorted positive triple indices (at most six indices, each at most 220); no hash collision can identify unequal integer keys.

If z's actual fixed signature is absent, z cannot have degree 15. This is necessary because any completed nonremovable degree-15 link at z is covered by the representative enumeration with x as the zero-weight point, and the marked y maps to one of the twelve enumerated choices. Three points with absent signatures would require at least three surplus incidences, contradicting the budget of two. This eliminates 16,333 of the 16,341 cases. All eight remaining cases force two specified degree-16 points, all others degree 15.

For each of these eight cases, the exact residual degree and lower bounds for triples and pairs give integer inequalities A_i u>=b_i, where u selects the 18 residual five-sets on Q. `data/last_eight_cert.json` supplies nonnegative integer weights c_i. For each possible five-set B, calculate s(B)=sum_i c_i A_i(B) and M=max_B s(B). The verifier checks all 792 possibilities and sum_i c_i b_i>18M. Any selection of 18 blocks would have summed score at most 18M and at least sum_i c_i b_i, a contradiction. All eight certificates pass. This proves the one-low branch impossible, including profiles containing weight 3. The numerical discovery of certificate weights is not relied on. No remaining irregular branch exists.

## 6. Exclusion of regular covers: high pair and triple multiplicities

If no low points exist, all degrees equal 15. The exact high-weight local enumeration yields pair codegrees at most seven. The proof in `regular_pair7/PROOF.md` and its verifier cover the ten normalized local links with a marked codegree-seven partner, enumerate 80 possible second links, exclude 56 by point budgets, map the other 24 by checked permutations to four cases, and verify five exact integer certificates (including an exhaustive integer split in one case). Hence pair codegrees are at most six.

For any pair P, the total excess of triples containing P equals 3(lambda(P)-4), because there are 12 such triples. If a triple occurs at least five times, all its pairs must have codegree six. The local classification in Section 4 independently confirms that the only possible link containing a pair of multiplicity at least five, among profiles of maximum weight two, has profile (1,1,1,1,2,2), multiplicity exactly five, and both endpoints of weight two. `regular_triple5/rare_triple.py` enumerates all such marked first links and their compatible second links (two marked representatives, eight continuations each). In every two-link union two pairs are already at codegree six and still have at least four uncovered triples. Each additional block can cover only three of them through a pair, forcing final codegree at least eight. Thus all regular triple multiplicities are at most four. The earlier detailed argument is in `regular_triple5/PROOF.md`.

## 7. Exclusion of every remaining regular pair of codegree six

If any degree-15 point has three codegree-six partners, its profile is (1,1,2,2,2). If two, it is (1,1,1,1,2,2). Four partners were excluded in Section 4. For each of the 29 or 161 representatives, choose a partner of codegree six (deterministically, using the largest local lower-bound sum). Its six known blocks must extend to a full 15-block link by nine further blocks avoiding the first root. Enumerate all weights 0,1,2 on its other twelve points, summing to six, and enumerate all exact-pair-cover completions with prescribed degrees, as in Section 4. A repeated fixed pair requires positive weights at its endpoints. If any point cannot meet its degree bounds, discard the assignment. No symmetry of a full cover is assumed. A fixed pair through the first root already has all triples covered by that root's complete link. The resulting unions have 24 blocks. Apply the necessary global pair/triple upper caps and the residual pair and point bounds used earlier (now exact degree 15). `core/regular_double3.cpp` gives 2792 completions with no surviving case for three partners, and 15792 completions with 57 surviving cases for two partners. Verify the 57 integer certificates in `data/regular_111122_dual.json`, by the same weighted-score inequality as Section 5, adding upper pair/triple bounds and exact degrees. All are excluded.

Therefore any codegree-six edges form a matching. If one exists, choose an endpoint and its unique partner. There are 333 normalized first links with profile (1,1,1,1,1,1,2). Repeat the second-link enumeration, now restricting the partner's other weights to 0 or 1. Use the matching condition also in the residual pair-degree bounds. `core/regular_matching.cpp` enumerates 29708 completions, of which 2335 pass those bounds. These are excluded by the exact propagation and search in Section 8: 1863 by propagation, the remaining 472 by complete branching (21630 states, zero completions).

## 8. Exact residual constraint propagation and branching

This method is used for both the preceding matching branch and the final branch. For any partial union F of two full links, the residual blocks avoid both roots and are distinct five-subsets of twelve remaining points. For every subset S of size 0,1,2,3 of those points, define r(S) to count yet-unselected residual blocks containing S. Maintain integer lower and upper bounds l(S),u(S) for r(S). Initial bounds express the remaining block budget, exact point degrees, pair codegree range, and triple coverage and upper cap. For each S and j=|S|+1,...,3,

    sum_{T superset S, |T|=j} r(T) = binom(5-|S|,j-|S|) * r(S).

These identities follow block by block, including S empty. Integer interval propagation on the equations gives new necessary lower and upper bounds (round inward for divisions). A candidate containing any S with u(S)=0 is forbidden. In an equation with multiplier m, at most m*u(S)-sum_T l(T) incidences can be spent on T with lower bound zero. Thus a candidate through S containing more such T than this slack is impossible. If l(S) equals the number of remaining candidate blocks containing S, all of those candidates are mandatory; add them, remove them from the unselected pool, and decrement bounds accordingly. The candidate count also supplies an upper bound. Further, if at most k of the still-required triple incidences through S can be supplied per active candidate containing S, the number of such blocks is at least ceil(total required incidences/k). These tests are necessary. In the matching branch, a pair forced to have final codegree six forces the upper codegree of all its incident competing pairs to five. All these tests are implemented with integer counts, not floating-point tolerances.

If any lower bound exceeds an upper bound, reject. Otherwise iterate the tests to a fixed point. This is finite: bounds and the active candidate pool only tighten, and mandatory selections are removed. If unresolved, choose a subset with positive lower bound and few active candidates, branch over all such candidates by adding one block. Every feasible completion must choose at least one of them. The search can revisit a set of blocks in different orders but cannot miss a feasible completion. Each recursion adds at least one distinct block, so depth is bounded by the remaining block budget. No numerical solver or unverified timeout is used for an exclusion. A candidate full cover is checked for all triples. `core/propagate.cpp` and `core/propagate_search.cpp` implement the matching branch. `core/propagate_simple.cpp` and `core/search_simple.cpp` implement the final branch, with the caps below.

## 9. Final regular branch: pair multiplicities four and five

Now every pair has codegree four or five. Every triple has multiplicity at most three: if abc occurs four times, all three of its pairs have codegree five. The four blocks through abc contain eight distinct further points, since the entire excess of each pair is already used by abc. The remaining three points form R={u,v,w}. The unique additional block for each of ab,ac,bc must contain R, to cover its three missing triples. These blocks are abR, acR, bcR. For pair uv, the triples uva,uvb,uvc occur at least twice and uvw at least three times, giving excess at least 5, contrary to the budget 3. A larger multiplicity already contradicts the pair excess budget. Hence the cap is three.

All first links have eight unit weights (219 normalized representatives). Choose any marked codegree-five partner, using the same deterministic rule as before; there are five shared blocks and ten further link blocks are needed. Its remaining weights are 0 or 1 with sum seven. `core/regular_simple.cpp` enumerates all such second links for every representative, applying the pair cap five, triple cap three, exact point degrees and residual incidence bounds. The original computation, split at representative 137, was subsequently reproduced in a single uninterrupted fresh run. It visits all 219 representatives and yields 6306 surviving unions of 25 blocks and 2,544,816 second-link completions. The fresh run uses no intermediate split log as input. There are 17 remaining blocks on the twelve nonroots. The integer propagation algorithm of Section 8 rejects 6289 unions. Complete branching rejects the remaining 17, visiting 110 states, no timeout and no cover. This excludes the last possible degree profile.

## 10. Conclusion and reproducibility

All possible numbers of low points have been exhausted: three or more, two, one, and zero. No covering of 42 blocks exists. A 43-block covering is provided and verified. Therefore the mathematical argument and finite enumerations give the proposed equality C(14,5,3)=43, subject to verification of the completeness and correctness of the reductions and code. This is a computer-assisted proof draft, not a peer-reviewed publication or a formal proof in Lean.

The archive contains a standard-library Python/C++17 verification driver, exact certificate data, the earlier reduction proofs and logs. Run `python verify.py` from the archive root with assertions enabled and a C++17 compiler. The discovery solver is not required. Individual stages can be run separately as explained in README.md. For slower hardware allow the verification to finish; an interruption is not a proof. The included logs distinguish original complete split runs from the interrupted combined control run. The one-low branch was freshly regenerated from the packaged sources and its eight certificates independently verified; the final 472 and 17 search cases were independently rerun from the package with the same results. The three-low, two-low, pair-seven and triple-five dependencies were also rerun in individual checks. A fresh all-in-one run of the original verifier has now completed successfully. The additional strengthened assertions were checked against the regenerated data or rerun directly, and `core/independent_residual.cpp` independently rejects the 2,335 matching-branch and 6,306 simple-branch unions. Run `python audit.py` to reproduce the combined original and new verification checks; see `AUDIT.md` for scope and limitations. No claim of global priority is made. Independent expert review or formal verification of the complete proof is strongly recommended before publication.
