# Excluding pair codegree seven in regular 42-block (14,5,3) coverings

> Historical proof-stage note. The current argument and supported reproduction commands are in [the main proof](../PROOF.md) and [the reproduction guide](../REPRODUCIBILITY.md). Earlier exploratory scripts mentioned in this note may not be included and are not required by the current verifier.

Research draft, 24 November 2026. Computer-checkable finite proof; independent expert review and priority are pending. This is not a proof of C(14,5,3)=43.

## Theorem
If a (14,5,3) covering has exactly 42 distinct blocks and every point belongs to exactly 15 blocks, then every pair belongs to at most six blocks.

## Proof
For a point x, its link is a family of 15 distinct four-sets covering all pairs of the other 13 points. A link point z has degree r(z)>=4, since its 12 partners must be covered three at a time. The excesses w(z)=r(z)-4 have sum 60-52=8. At least one link point has excess zero. Every pair involving that point is covered exactly once. Relabel the zero-excess point as 0; its four incident four-sets give a partition of the remaining 12 points into four triples G. Permutations of the triples and of the labels within them permit sorting the weight multisets within and between the triples. Thus the integer partitions of eight and the normalized triple layouts exhaust all possibilities. To test whether any pair has codegree at least seven, only partitions containing a part at least three need be considered.

`verify.py` regenerates every one of these 98 layouts. The exact recursion in `degree15_full_links.py` chooses an uncovered pair involving a zero-excess point and branches over every allowable four-set that covers that pair without repeating any such pair. Pairs with two positive-excess endpoints need only be covered. The recursion keeps the prescribed positive-point degrees and the remaining block budget, and enumerates all possible distinct all-positive blocks at the end. These rules are necessary for every actual link and do not discard any valid completion. No numerical optimization is involved. The only feasible layouts are four with positive weight multisets (1,1,1,2,3) or (1,1,1,1,1,3). The enumeration produces 208 distinct labelled links within those normalized layouts, and explicitly applies the weight-preserving group of triple permutations and internal permutations to reduce them to 10 representatives. In particular no weight greater than three is possible.

For each representative, let x be the parent point (label 13) and y its unique codegree-seven partner. Exactly seven parent blocks through x also contain y. They are fixed in the link at y, and the remaining eight link blocks at y must avoid x. Since d(y)=15, the same classification applies to its positive weights: the weight of x is three and the other positive weights have multiset (1,1,1,2) or (1,1,1,1,1). `degree15_pair7_extensions.py` enumerates all assignments of these weights to the other 12 points, applies necessary point-degree and repeated-pair tests, and enumerates all eight-block extensions by the same exact-cover recursion. The union of both parent links has 23 distinct blocks. This procedure finds 80 second-link completions over the 10 representatives.

For such a union F, define h(P) as the number of triples containing pair P that F has not covered, p(P) as the pair degree in F, and d_F(z) as its point degree. In any completion by 19 more blocks, the residual pair count is at least q(P)=max(0,4-p(P),ceil(h(P)/3)). The residual degree of z is therefore at least ceil(sum_{P containing z} q(P)/4), and it must equal 15-d_F(z). This excludes 56 completions by integer arithmetic. The remaining 24 map to four stored representatives by the explicit permutations in `orbit_maps.json`; the verifier checks every permutation and every mapped block exactly, allowing interchange of the two roots. Hence no unexamined case is omitted.

For each of the four representatives, all 19 remaining blocks are selected among the 792 five-sets on the twelve points other than x,y. Let u_B in {0,1} denote a selection. Necessary linear inequalities encode the exact block budget 19, exact residual degrees 15-d_F(z), total pair degrees between four and seven, and coverage of every missing triple. The constraints are reconstructed by `verify.py` in a deterministic order, including u_B<=1 and implicit u_B>=0. Each row has integer coefficients and is written A_i u>=b_i.

Three cases have direct nonnegative integer multipliers c_i with sum c_i A_i<=0 componentwise and sum c_i b_i>0. Such a certificate contradicts u>=0. The fourth case splits on the integer residual codegree of pair (1,8), which must be <=3 or >=4. Both branches have certificates of exactly the same form. All five certificates are checked for every candidate block using only exact Python integers. The discovery of the multipliers by floating-point linear programming is not part of the proof; the resulting integer inequalities are checked without any numerical solver.

Every candidate regular covering with some pair codegree >=7 has been reduced to one of these impossible cases. Therefore all its pair codegrees are <=6. QED.

## Reproduce
Run `python verify.py` with Python 3.10+ and assertions enabled. Standard library only. `verification.log` records a successful run. `pair7_lp_exact.py` and `pair7_regular_milp.py` are optional discovery scripts requiring SciPy/NumPy; their output is not relied upon by the verifier. `degree15_full_links.py` is imported by the verifier, which enumerates all high-weight layouts directly; its optional standalone main reads the earlier layout classification and is not needed. The five certificates, full-link representatives, second-link completions, and orbit maps are included.

## Scope
Only the regular 42-block case is addressed. Regular coverings with every pair codegree <=6 and nonregular coverings remain unresolved. Neither exact C(14,5,3) nor scientific priority is asserted. The case-reduction and certificate strategy was motivated by the public proof package for C(12,6,4)=41 (https://github.com/dennisonbertram/covering-number-c12-6-4) and Requeijo's minimum-link methodology (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31696).
